# Antim Sahara ("The Last Will") — Build Research Dossier

## TL;DR
- **It's buildable and it's white space.** Every legal/procedural step a grieving Indian family faces (death registration → bank/EPFO/insurance/mutual-fund claims → succession documents → tracing unclaimed money) is governed by current, officially-documented rules (RBI Directions 2025, EPFO forms, IRDAI circulars, Indian Succession Act 1925, the crsorgi Civil Registration System) — and no Indian player offers an agentic, India-specific *post-death concierge*. Indian startups (Yellow, WillJini) focus on *pre-death* will-writing; Western apps (Empathy, Cake) are US-probate-specific.
- **The Google/Gemini/Firebase stack fits the hackathon perfectly.** Use Google ADK (multi-agent: Sequential/Parallel/Loop workflow agents) on Vertex AI Agent Engine or Cloud Run; Gemini 3 Flash for multimodal document OCR + structured JSON output + function-calling; Firebase Auth/Firestore/Storage with DPDP-compliant encryption; Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn/ui front-end; Bhashini for vernacular ASR/TTS/translation with Gemini multilingual as fallback.
- **The killer feature is the case "roadmap" as a state machine** that branches by religion (Hindu/Muslim/Christian/Parsi), asset type, will/no-will, and nominee status — plus a master document vault so a death certificate uploaded once is reused across every claim.

## Key Findings

1. **Death registration is the gateway document and is now centralised.** Under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969 (as amended by Act No. 20 of 2023, in force from Oct 1, 2023), death must be registered within 21 days, free, at the municipal/panchayat registrar or via the CRS portal (crsorgi.gov.in / dc.crsorgi.gov.in). Hospitals issue the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death (MCCD, Form 4/4A); home deaths use Form 4A by an attending practitioner or a councillor/headman certificate. The amendment makes the digital certificate the single accepted proof of date/fact of death and builds a national database.

2. **The RBI completely standardised deceased-account settlement in 2025.** The "RBI (Settlement of Claims in respect of Deceased Customers of Banks) Directions, 2025" were issued on September 26, 2025 (Notification No. RBI/2025-26/82), with mandatory implementation by all commercial and co-operative banks no later than March 31, 2026, resting on Sections 45ZA–45ZF read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949. They confirm: nominee/"either or survivor" accounts pay out with just claim form + death certificate + ID (no succession certificate, irrespective of amount); no-nominee accounts use a simplified procedure within a threshold of ₹15 lakh (commercial banks) / ₹5 lakh (co-operative banks); above that, succession certificate/probate is required. Standardised Annexure forms (I-A to I-H), online lodging, settlement within 15 calendar days of receiving all documents, and delay-compensation (Bank Rate + 4% p.a. on deposits; ₹5,000/day for lockers) now apply.

3. **Four different "who inherits / who can collect" instruments exist and are constantly confused** — the app's single biggest value-add is routing users to the right one:
   - **Legal Heir Certificate** — revenue authority (Tahsildar/Village Officer/SDM), administrative, identifies heirs, used for pension/PF/utility transfers; ~15–30 days.
   - **Succession Certificate** — District Civil Court, Indian Succession Act 1925 ss.370–390, for *debts & securities only* (bank deposits, shares, MFs); newspaper notice + objection window; months.
   - **Probate** — court-certified will, mandatory for wills in the presidency-towns (Mumbai/Chennai/Kolkata) under ss.57/213.
   - **Letters of Administration** — court grant when there's no will or no executor.

4. **EPFO death benefits are three parallel claims** (Form 20 = PF balance; Form 10D = EPS monthly family pension; Form 5IF = EDLI insurance, min ₹2.5 lakh / max ₹7 lakh), via the last employer; gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act is payable even if service was under 5 years when death is the cause.

5. **Insurance death claims** follow IRDAI norms (claim form, death certificate, policy bond, ID); IRDAI's 2024 master circular sets settlement timelines and an interest penalty for delay; unclaimed amounts older than 10 years go to the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund (claimable for 25 years), searchable on IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal.

6. **Tracing unclaimed money is now feasible online** — RBI's UDGAM portal (udgam.rbi.org.in) for unclaimed bank deposits (DEA Fund ₹72,454 crore as of Jan 28, 2026); IEPF Form IEPF-5 (iepf.gov.in) for unclaimed shares/dividends; insurer websites + Bima Bharosa for unclaimed policies.

## Details

### PART A — Indian legal & procedural domain (guidance, not legal advice; flag state/institution variation)

**A1. Death registration.** Statutory basis: Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969, amended by Act No. 20 of 2023 (assent June 2023; provisions in force from Oct 1, 2023). Key rules: register within 21 days of death (free); 22 days–1 year requires District Registrar permission + late fee; beyond 1 year requires a District Magistrate/Executive Magistrate order + affidavit. Who reports: hospital medical officer (institutional deaths, issues MCCD Form 4); attending practitioner (home death, Form 4A); jail in-charge (prison); police (unnatural/public-place deaths). Where: local municipal body / panchayat registrar, or online via CRS portal crsorgi.gov.in (now mandatory for states to use). Documents: death report form (Form 2), MCCD/cremation-burial proof, deceased's Aadhaar + ID, applicant ID, affidavit if delayed. Issuance typically 7–15 days; the digital signed PDF is legally valid. **Flag:** state portals differ (e.g., e-Nagarsewa in UP); some states (Maharashtra) require info within 72 hours to municipal authority; the CRS portal is rolled out state-by-state. The 2023 amendment also collects Aadhaar of the informant and adds an appeal mechanism (30 days to District Registrar; decision in 90 days).

**A2. Bank accounts.** Governing instrument: **RBI (Settlement of Claims in respect of Deceased Customers of Banks) Directions, 2025** (issued Sept 26, 2025; Notification RBI/2025-26/82; full implementation by March 31, 2026; consolidates the older June 9, 2005 simplification circular and the Master Circular on Customer Service). Rules:
- **Nominee / "Either or Survivor" / survivorship clause:** bank pays the survivor/nominee on claim form + death certificate + ID, treated as full discharge; **no** succession certificate, probate, or indemnity bond, irrespective of amount. In joint accounts the nominee's right arises only after death of all holders.
- **No nominee / no survivorship clause:** simplified procedure within a **threshold (minimum ₹15 lakh for commercial banks; ₹5 lakh for co-operative banks, or higher if the bank fixes it)** — claim form (Annex I-B), death certificate, ID, indemnity bond (Annex I-C), disclaimer/NOC from non-claimant heirs (Annex I-D), and a legal heir certificate or affidavit/declaration (Annex I-E).
- **Above threshold:** succession certificate / probate / LoA / court decree, plus affidavit sworn before a magistrate and possibly third-party indemnity/surety.
- **Will (undisputed):** bank may act on it without probate if genuine and all heirs agree; if disputed, probate/court order required.
- **Missing person:** civil-death order under Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023; for small claims (up to ₹1 lakh) FIR + non-traceable police report may substitute.
- **Frozen account:** an account becomes inoperative/frozen on death until claim settled; the nominee route unfreezes fastest.
- **Settlement timeline & compensation:** within 15 calendar days of receiving all documents; delay attracts Bank Rate + 4% p.a. on deposits and ₹5,000/day for locker delays.
- Banks must accept **online claim lodging** and publish forms at every branch and website. **Flag:** the exact threshold above ₹15 lakh is bank-specific — re-verify per bank.
- Context: Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025 + Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules 2025 (effective Nov 1, 2025) now allow up to **four nominees** per account (successive or simultaneous).

**A3. Succession Certificate.** Indian Succession Act 1925, Part X, ss.370–390. Scope: **debts and securities only** (bank deposits, FDs, shares, debentures, mutual funds, bonds) — NOT immovable property. Court: District Judge where deceased ordinarily resided, or where assets are located (s.371); NRIs/OCIs may apply. Process (s.372): petition stating time of death, residence, family/heirs, and the debts/securities schedule; court issues a newspaper notice inviting objections (~30–45 day window); after objections resolved, court fee (judicial stamp) paid and certificate granted. The certificate protects debtors who pay the holder (s.381) but does **not** establish title/heirship. Cannot be granted where probate/LoA is required (s.370). **Court fee** is an ad valorem percentage of the value of debts/securities under the Court Fees Act 1870 (Article 12, Sch. I) as amended by each state — commonly ~2–3%, with state caps:
  - **Maharashtra:** 7.5% capped at ₹75,000 (slabs: 2% up to ₹50k; 4% ₹50k–₹2L; 6% ₹2–3L; 7.5% above ₹3L). 2024 relief: maximum fee for *widows* on probate/LoA reduced from ₹75,000 to ₹10,000.
  - **West Bengal:** capped at ₹50,000 regardless of value.
  - **Karnataka:** linked to the probate scale (Karnataka Court Fees & Suits Valuation Act 1958), effectively ~5% capped around ₹30,000 on the relevant slab.
  - **Tamil Nadu:** 2% up to ₹5,000, 3% above (TN Court-Fees & Suits Valuation Act 1955); a constitutional challenge to fees beyond ₹25,000 has been litigated.
  - **Delhi:** base Court Fees Act 1870 rates apply (the 2012 Delhi Amendment was struck down in 2013); secondary sources cite 2%/2.5%/3% — unsettled, re-verify.
  - **Kerala / Gujarat:** Kerala ad valorem under its 1959 Act (≈2–3%); Gujarat 7.5% capped at ₹75,000.
  - **Timeline:** roughly 4–7 months uncontested (2–6 months if no objections). **Flag:** percentages/caps must be re-verified against each state's amended Court-Fees Act PDF on indiacode.nic.in.

**A4. Distinguishing the four instruments.**

| Instrument | Issued by | Purpose | Assets | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legal Heir Certificate | Revenue (Tahsildar/Village Officer/SDM) | Identifies surviving heirs (administrative) | Pension, PF, gratuity, utilities, compassionate jobs | Quick, ~15–30 days; not proof of title |
| Succession Certificate | District Civil Court | Authority to collect debts/securities | Bank deposits, shares, MFs, bonds | No will + no nominee; financial assets |
| Probate | District/High Court | Certifies a will + executor's authority | Estate under the will | Will exists; mandatory in presidency towns / high-value/immovable |
| Letters of Administration | District/High Court | Authority to administer estate | Whole estate | No will, or will without executor |

A registered will changes the path: heirs apply for probate (or LoA if no executor) rather than a succession certificate; banks may release without probate if the will is undisputed (RBI 2025). Probate is mandatory for wills made/property within the ordinary civil jurisdiction of the Bombay, Madras, and Calcutta High Courts (ss.57/213); for Hindus elsewhere, probate is often not legally mandatory but increasingly demanded by banks/sub-registrars.

**A5. Religion-based intestate succession (the app must branch).**
- **Hindus/Buddhists/Jains/Sikhs:** Hindu Succession Act 1956. Class I heirs inherit simultaneously and equally and exclude Class II: son, daughter, widow, mother, plus heirs of pre-deceased children. The 2005 amendment made daughters coparceners by birth in Mitakshara joint-family property, equal to sons (abolishing survivorship for coparcenary; *Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma* confirmed rights regardless of birth date or whether the father was alive in 2005). A 2026 Supreme Court ruling confirmed the 2005 amendment does not limit daughters' pre-existing Class-I rights.
- **Muslims:** uncodified personal law (Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937), Sunni/Shia rules; heirs are sharers + residuaries (+ distant kindred for Shia). Examples: widow gets 1/8 with children, 1/4 without; husband 1/4 with children, 1/2 without; each parent 1/6 with children; daughter gets half a son's share. Testamentary freedom is capped at 1/3 (wasiyyat); Sunni law bars a bequest to an existing heir without other heirs' consent.
- **Christians/Parsis/Jews & others:** Indian Succession Act 1925 (intestate). Christian (s.33): spouse 1/3 + lineal descendants 2/3; no descendants → spouse 1/2 + kindred 1/2; no kindred → spouse takes all. Parsi (ss.50–53): widow/widower and each child take equal shares; parents get half a child's share. The Indian Succession Act 1925 also governs *testamentary* succession for all communities except Muslims.

**A6. EPFO + gratuity.** A member's death triggers three claims, routed through the last employer (each page signed by employer; if establishment closed, attested by bank manager/MP/MLA/magistrate):
- **EPF balance — Form 20** (nominee/legal heir; processed ~10–20 days).
- **EPS family pension — Form 10D** (widow/widower for life unless remarriage; children up to 25; nominee must have a NACH-enabled bank account).
- **EDLI insurance — Form 5IF:** payout = 35× average monthly wage of the last 12 months + bonus, **min ₹2.5 lakh, max ₹7 lakh** (the min ₹2.5 lakh / max ₹7 lakh limits were set by EPFO's Central Board of Trustees in April 2024, raised from ₹6 lakh); no minimum service; covers death within 6 months of the last contribution; tax-free under s.10(10D). A **Composite Death Claim Form** combines 20/10D/5IF. Documents: death certificate, claimant ID, bank proof, guardianship certificate for minors. **Flag:** the EPFO nominee and the legal heir may differ; nomination (Form 2/11) may be outdated. **Gratuity** (Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, s.4): payable to nominee/heir; the 5-year minimum service is waived when death is the cause.

**A7. Insurance.** LIC/IRDAI death-claim documents: claim form, original policy bond, death certificate, claimant ID/bank proof; early/accidental deaths need extra investigation. IRDAI's 2024 Protection of Policyholders' Interests master circular sets settlement timelines and an interest penalty (Bank Rate + 2%) for delay, plus a 30-day free-look. Unclaimed policy amounts (unclaimed >6 months after due) must be displayed on insurer websites for amounts ≥₹1,000; amounts unclaimed >10 years (as on Sept 30) transfer to the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund by March 1, claimable for up to 25 years. Search via **Bima Bharosa** (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in) with policy no./PAN/name/DOB. Industry unclaimed pool reported around ₹22,000–25,000 crore (FY24). **Flag:** figures are reported estimates; re-verify.

**A8. Mutual fund / demat transmission.** Transmission (not a sale; no capital-gains tax at transmission). Process via DP / RTA (CAMS/KFintech for MFs; NSDL/CDSL for demat):
- **Nominee/joint holder:** Transmission Request Form (TRF) + notarised/attested death certificate + nominee KYC (PAN, CMR/CML) → ~7 working days (Zerodha/Axis quote 7 days, no charge).
- **No nominee, sole holding:** value-based threshold set by DP (CDSL FAQ cites ₹5 lakh) — below it, TRF + death certificate + affidavit + indemnity bond + NOC from other heirs + optional family settlement deed; above it, succession certificate/probate/LoA. SEBI raised simplified-documentation thresholds (April 2022): **₹5 lakh per listed issuer (physical)** and **₹15 lakh per beneficiary account (demat)**; above these, succession certificate/probate is required. A Legal Heir Certificate from a Tahsildar-level authority is acceptable for simplified transmission. (Older ₹2 lakh references are superseded.)
- **IEPF (unclaimed shares/dividends):** if shares/dividends were transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund, the legal heir files **Form IEPF-5** online (iepf.gov.in) under Section 125(3)(a), Companies Act 2013 + Rule 7(1), IEPF Rules 2016 (PAN-verified; physical forms rejected). Steps: complete *transmission with the company first* (death certificate; Legal Heir Certificate if value <₹5 lakh or Succession Certificate/probate if >₹5 lakh; affidavit; indemnity bond; NOC from co-heirs) → file IEPF-5 (generates SRN) → send originals (indemnity bond, advance stamped receipt, share certificate/Demat statement, Aadhaar, entitlement proof, cancelled cheque) to the company's **Nodal Officer** (a Director/CFO/Company Secretary) → company sends a **verification report** to the Authority (Rule 7(3): within 15 days) with an entitlement letter → **Authority disposes within 60 days** of a complete verification report. (Note: some sources cite a 30-day company deadline with ₹50/day penalty — re-verify.)

**A9. Government / family pension + Jeevan Pramaan.** Central-govt family pension under CCS (Pension) Rules; spouse first claimant (for life unless disqualified), then eligible children. Defence has liberalised/enhanced family pension. Forms (family pension on death, death gratuity) downloadable from doppw.gov.in; submitted to the pension-sanctioning authority/bank. **Jeevan Pramaan** (jeevanpramaan.gov.in) is the Aadhaar-biometric/face-auth Digital Life Certificate that *living* pensioners (incl. family pensioners) submit annually (super-seniors from Oct 1; others Nov 1; deadline Nov 30) — relevant because after the primary pensioner's death, the family pensioner must start submitting their own DLC to continue the family pension.

**A10. Master document checklist (reused across all claims).** Death certificate (multiple certified copies — the single most-reused document); deceased Aadhaar/PAN; applicant/claimant Aadhaar/PAN + photo; relationship/heirship proof (Legal Heir Certificate / ration card / family tree); bank proof (cancelled cheque/passbook); nomination forms; affidavits (sworn before magistrate/notary); indemnity bonds (on appropriate non-judicial stamp paper); NOC/disclaimer from co-heirs; and—above thresholds—succession certificate/probate/LoA. The app's document vault should store each once and auto-attach to each institution's claim packet.

### PART B — Market / differentiation

- **Empathy** (US, founded 2021 by CEO Ron Gura and Yonatan Bergman): "Headspace for grief + TurboTax for estate settlement"; total capital raised now ~**$162M** after a $72M Series C in 2025 led by Adams Street Partners (on top of the $47M Series B led by Index Ventures announced March 12, 2024, with strategic investors MetLife, New York Life, MassMutual, Securian, Sumitomo). Coverage is now "over 50 million covered lives," and "one in five life insurance claims in the U.S. includes Empathy's support." Mostly B2B2C via employers/insurers (together 99% of business); combines AI + a human care team; cites that winding down a deceased's affairs takes 420+ hours on average (US deaths >3 million annually in 2023, per US Census).
- **Cake** (US, founded 2014, Suelin Chen): end-of-life planning platform; B2B2C via health systems/insurers/banks; "touches half of all deaths in the U.S." Others: Lantern, Everplans, Trust & Will, Farewill (UK).
- **India — pre-death only:** **Yellow** (getyellow.in, Digital Succession Solutions Pvt Ltd, Bengaluru; Google for Startups Accelerator 2024; SAGE-recognised) — fully digital wills, trusts, PoA, gift deeds + post-demise asset-transfer support; ₹46 lakh revenue FY24; targets a ~$2.8 Bn market. **WillJini** (since 2014, Mumbai; channel partners HDFC, ICICI, Tata AIA, Muthoot, Union Bank) — online wills, probate, succession certificate, family trusts. Others: AasaanWill, Willstar, Digiwill, FreeWill.
- **The gap / white space:** Indian players are overwhelmingly *will-writing and estate-planning (pre-death)*; none is an *agentic, post-death, India-specific concierge* that walks a grieving family through registration → bank/EPFO/insurance/MF claims → succession documents → unclaimed-money tracing, branching by religion and asset type, in vernacular languages. Context: <10% of Indians have a will (vs ~46% in the US); ₹50,000 crore+ in unclaimed assets — a large, underserved, emotionally fraught market. (Note: this is a hackathon project, not a commercial launch.)

### PART C — Technical implementation (Google/Gemini/Firebase)

**C1. Agentic architecture — Google ADK.** ADK (open-sourced at Google Cloud Next April 2025; Python/TypeScript/Go/Java/Kotlin; Apache 2.0; ADK Python 2.0 GA with graph workflows) is model-agnostic but optimised for Gemini/Vertex. Use its three **workflow agents** as composition primitives: **SequentialAgent** (ordered pipeline — e.g., DocParser → ClaimRouter → DocGenerator), **ParallelAgent** (run independent claim tracks concurrently — bank + EPFO + insurance), **LoopAgent** (refine until a checklist condition holds). Workflow agents nest (they are themselves agents). Pattern: a planner/orchestrator (Gemini 3 Pro) routes to specialised sub-agents (death-registration agent, bank-claim agent, succession agent, unclaimed-tracing agent), each with typed tools (function calling). Built-in tools include google_search, code_execution, vertex_ai_search. **Deploy** to Vertex AI Agent Engine (managed runtime — autoscaling, sessions, Memory Bank), Cloud Run, or GKE. For a hackathon, Cloud Run (container) or Agent Engine via the ADK quickstart is fastest; Vertex AI Express Mode gives limited free quota.

**C2. Gemini API.** Use **Gemini 3 Flash (gemini-3-flash-preview)** — has a free tier in the Gemini Developer API, 1M-token input context, multimodal (text, images, PDFs, audio, video) — for document OCR/understanding (read death certificates, passbooks, policy PDFs). Use **structured output** (responseMimeType application/json + responseSchema; Pydantic/Zod supported; propertyOrdering matters) to extract fields into your case schema. Gemini 3 can combine structured output with function calling + Google Search grounding. For drafting documents use the same models. **Risks/flags:** free-tier rate limits are per-project RPM/TPM/RPD (429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED on exceed); Gemini 2.0 Flash/Flash-Lite shut down June 1, 2026, and all Imagen models June 24, 2026 — use current 3.x models; structured output guarantees syntax not semantics (validate in code). Knowledge cutoff Jan 2025 — use Search grounding for current rules.

**C3. Document generation (PDFs/DOCX).** Templated generation in a React + Node/Firebase stack: use a templating library to fill succession-petition drafts, affidavits, indemnity bonds, and per-institution claim cover letters. Common approaches: server-side HTML→PDF, a PDF library (e.g., pdf-lib / react-pdf), or DOCX templating with placeholders (e.g., docxtemplater); alternatively the Google Docs API (template doc + batchUpdate text-replacement → export PDF). Gemini fills the variable text; the template enforces legal formatting. Store generated files in Cloud Storage.

**C4. Bhashini (vernacular).** Government of India (MeitY) National Language Translation Mission; free APIs for ASR, NMT (translation), TTS across 22 scheduled Indian languages, exposed via the ULCA pipeline model (Pipeline Search → Pipeline Config → compute, using service/pipeline IDs). **Caveats:** PoC-oriented; audio expected as Base64 WAV (WebM from browsers must be converted); no built-in profanity filter; reliability/latency vary. **Fallback:** Gemini multilingual for translation/understanding and TTS where Bhashini is flaky. Strategy: Bhashini for authentic Indic ASR/TTS, Gemini for reasoning/translation.

**C5. Firebase + DPDP.** Auth: phone (OTP) + email. Firestore data model: top-level `cases/{caseId}` with sub-collections `documents`, `tasks`, `heirs`, `claims`, `assets`; per-user access. Cloud Storage for sensitive uploads (death certs, passbooks). **Security Rules** scope reads/writes to the authenticated owner/collaborators. Cloud Functions for server-side claim logic and Gemini calls (keep API keys server-side). **DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025** (notified Nov 13, 2025; operative provisions phasing through ~2026–2027): you are a *Data Fiduciary*; Rule 6 mandates "reasonable security safeguards" — **encryption** (at rest + in transit), obfuscation/masking/virtual tokens, role-based access control, access logs/monitoring (retain ~1 year), breach notification to the Data Protection Board within 72 hours, and data-erasure obligations. DPDP treats all personal data uniformly (no separate "sensitive" tier) but death/financial data is high-risk; penalties up to ₹250 crore. For a hackathon: encrypt, restrict rules, minimise data, and show a consent screen + clear privacy notice.

**C6. React front-end.** Next.js (App Router) + Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui components + Framer Motion/Motion for gentle, compassionate transitions. Accessibility: large tap targets, high contrast, screen-reader labels, simple language, a vernacular toggle, low-literacy-friendly icons, and voice input/output (Bhashini). Compassionate UX: show only the next relevant step, "what can wait," and progress reassurance.

**C7. Roadmap as data (state machine).** Represent each case as a checklist/state machine: tasks with `id, title, status (todo/blocked/in_progress/done), dependencies[], deadline, requiredDocs[], institution, reuseDocs[]`. Branch logic by religion, will/no-will, nominee/no-nominee, asset types, and threshold values. Track deadlines (21-day registration; EPFO/insurance windows) and document reuse (link each task's requiredDocs to the central vault). This data structure is what the ADK agents read/write and what the UI renders.

**C8. Deployment & reliable demo.** Firebase Hosting (Next.js front-end) + Cloud Run (agent/back-end) + Vertex AI Agent Engine (agents). Secrets via Google Secret Manager / environment config (never in client). **Demo reliability:** seed Firestore with a fictional deceased persona (the "Antim Sahara" demo case) and fixture documents; **cache/pre-compute AI outputs** for the demo path to avoid live-API rate-limit (429) failures during a live demo; provide a "demo mode" toggle that serves cached Gemini responses; keep a recorded fallback video.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Core MVP (hackathon-winning slice).** Build the case state machine + document vault + one fully working agentic flow end-to-end: upload death certificate (Gemini OCR → structured JSON) → branch by religion + nominee status → generate a personalised roadmap → produce one filled document (e.g., bank claim cover letter + indemnity bond) + a bank-claim checklist. Use Gemini 3 Flash, Firestore, Cloud Storage, Next.js/Tailwind/shadcn. This demonstrates the whole value proposition. **Build cached demo mode into Stage 1.**

**Stage 2 — Multi-agent + vernacular.** Add ParallelAgent tracks (bank + EPFO + insurance + unclaimed-tracing) and Bhashini voice/translation (Hindi first) with Gemini fallback. Add the UDGAM / Bima Bharosa / IEPF "trace unclaimed money" agent that explains how to search and produces a claim packet.

**Stage 3 — Hardening.** DPDP-style encryption, security rules, consent + privacy notice, audit logs; per-state court-fee logic; expand religion branches; add deadline reminders.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** if live Gemini rate limits threaten the demo → switch to cached demo mode. If Bhashini latency/quality is poor in testing → default to Gemini multilingual. If ADK setup eats too much time → start with a single Gemini function-calling loop and refactor into ADK workflow agents only if time remains. Always label outputs "guidance, not legal advice" and "verify figures/thresholds with the institution/state."

## Caveats
- This is a **guidance tool, not legal advice**; every figure, threshold, court fee, and timeline **varies by state and institution and must be re-verified** at build time against the primary source.
- Several monetary figures are **reported estimates** (DEA Fund ₹72,454 cr as of Jan 28, 2026 — public-sector banks ₹60,571.02 cr, private ₹9,607.76 cr, foreign ₹2,275.01 cr; unclaimed insurance ~₹22,000–25,000 cr; EDLI/threshold amounts) and the **RBI 2025 Directions, DPDP Rules 2025, and Banking Laws (Amendment) Act 2025 are newly in force** — confirm current text.
- **Court-fee percentages/caps** (Maharashtra ₹75,000 cap; WB ₹50,000 cap; Karnataka ~₹30,000; TN ~3%; Gujarat ₹75,000; Delhi unsettled) come partly from practitioner sources; cite each state's amended Court-Fees Act PDF (indiacode.nic.in) before publishing precise numbers.
- **IEPF verification timeline** conflicts across sources (Rule 7(3) statutory 15 days vs a 30-day FAQ figure with ₹50/day penalty; Authority disposal 60 days from complete verification report) — re-verify.
- **Gemini model names and free-tier limits change frequently**; verify current models (3.x) and quotas before the build, and account for the June 2026 deprecations of Gemini 2.0 and Imagen.
- This is a **hackathon project, not a commercial launch** — scope accordingly.
